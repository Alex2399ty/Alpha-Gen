Interest Rates
How Fed Rate Decisions Affect Your Portfolio
When the Fed raises rates, it costs more to borrow money. This is bad for growth stocks (they need cheap capital to grow) and good for banks. When the Fed cuts rates, growth stocks benefit because future earnings are worth more at a lower discount rate. Today's signal: 2 cuts expected in H2 2026 → bullish for NVDA, MSFT, PLTR, AMZN.
Earnings Calls
Reading Between the Lines of Earnings Calls
What gets said on earnings calls matters less than how it gets said. AlphaGen's agents analyze word confidence, hedging language frequency, and forward guidance precision. A CEO who gives specific numbers outperforms one who uses vague ranges by 12% over 3 years on average. We score every call automatically.
DCA Strategy
Why Dollar Cost Averaging Is The Best Strategy
DCA means investing a fixed amount at regular intervals regardless of price. When prices are low you automatically buy more shares. When prices are high you buy fewer. Over time this lowers your average cost per share and removes the impossible-to-win game of timing the market. Every crash becomes a buying opportunity.
Geopolitics
How World Events Map to Your Holdings
Taiwan tensions affect NVDA, AMD, and AAPL because TSMC manufactures their chips. Fed decisions affect growth stocks more than dividend payers. Oil price spikes benefit XOM but hurt consumer discretionary. AlphaGen maps every macro event to your specific holdings and explains the mechanism — not just the headline.
Compounding
The Eighth Wonder of the World
$300/month invested for 30 years at 10% average annual return becomes $658,000 — but you only put in $108,000 of your own money. The other $550,000 is pure compounding. The critical insight: time is more important than amount. Starting at 22 vs 32 can mean a $400,000 difference at retirement with the same monthly contribution.
Insider Trading
Legal Insider Buying: The Signal Nobody Watches
Executives must report stock purchases on SEC Form 4 within 2 days. When a CEO buys their own stock on the open market (not a pre-planned 10b5-1 program), it means they believe the stock is undervalued. Karp's $2.1M PLTR purchase is one of the strongest buy signals we track — non-10b5-1 executive purchases outperform the market by 8.2% on average over 12 months.
